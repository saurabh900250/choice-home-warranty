Why More Homeowners Are Looking at Home Warranty Plans Before Their Next Major Repair Bill

Air conditioning, plumbing, electrical systems and everyday appliances can fail unexpectedly. Here’s why some homeowners consider a home protection plan before that happens.

By Homeowner Daily Editorial Team Updated August 2026 • 6-minute read

Your refrigerator is working today. Your air conditioner is cooling the house. Your water heater seems fine. But household systems and appliances do not last forever, and an unexpected breakdown can quickly turn into an unplanned repair expense.

That is one reason some homeowners explore home warranty service contracts. A home warranty is different from homeowners insurance: it is generally designed to help with eligible repairs or replacements involving covered systems and appliances that fail because of normal wear and tear, subject to the contract terms, exclusions and limits.

Quick definition

A home warranty is a service contract that may help cover eligible repair or replacement costs for specified home systems and appliances, depending on the plan selected.

Why Some Homeowners Prefer a More Predictable Repair Process

Imagine your air conditioner stops cooling during the middle of summer. Without a protection plan, you may need to find a technician, arrange service and deal with the repair cost yourself.

With an eligible covered breakdown under a home warranty service contract, the process may be more structured: you submit a claim, pay the applicable service fee, and the provider coordinates service according to the contract terms.

Could a Home Warranty Make Sense for Your Home?

A home warranty may be worth comparing if several of the following situations sound familiar:

  • You own an existing home.
  • Some major appliances are several years old.
  • Your HVAC, plumbing or electrical systems are used heavily.
  • You prefer planning for household expenses rather than dealing with entirely unexpected repair costs.
  • You like having one place to request service for eligible covered problems.
