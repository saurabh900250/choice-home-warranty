What does Choice Home Warranty cover?

Choice Home Warranty offers service contracts for eligible home systems and appliances that stop working because of normal wear and tear. It is separate from homeowners insurance and from a manufacturer's warranty.

The practical difference

Homeowners insurance generally responds to covered events such as fire or theft. A home service contract can help with eligible mechanical breakdowns of listed systems and appliances.

What may not be covered?

A home service contract is not blanket protection for every home repair. Coverage depends on the exact cause of failure, the covered components and the current agreement.

  • Pre-existing, known or detectable conditions may be excluded
  • Damage from misuse, neglect or failure to perform maintenance may not qualify
  • Cosmetic defects and non-covered components are generally outside coverage
  • Structural items and problems normally handled by homeowners insurance are not the purpose of the plan
  • Coverage limits can apply per covered item and per agreement term
  • Choice Home Warranty may determine whether to repair, replace or offer cash in lieu, subject to the agreement
Read the current agreement

Do not rely only on a plan comparison table. Confirm the definitions, service fee, waiting period, exclusions, dollar limits and cash-in-lieu terms for your state before paying.

How a service request works

1

Submit a service request

Report the issue online or by phone. Claims access is available 24 hours a day.

2

Service professional is arranged

Choice Home Warranty works to assign a service professional, subject to provider availability and the agreement.

3

Pay the applicable service fee

The service fee shown in your coverage details is generally due for each service request.

4

Coverage decision and resolution

If the issue is eligible, repair, replacement or another contract-approved resolution may be authorised within applicable limits.

Who may find it useful?

  • Homeowners with ageing systems or appliances that are no longer under manufacturer warranty
  • Buyers who want a more predictable process for eligible breakdowns after moving in
  • Sellers who want to add a home protection option during a real estate transaction
  • People who prefer one service-request process instead of independently finding a technician each time

It may be less suitable if your systems are new and fully covered elsewhere, you can comfortably self-fund repairs, or the contract exclusions do not match your home’s likely risks.
